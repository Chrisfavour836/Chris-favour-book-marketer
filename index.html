Sound Familiar?

Marketing a book can feel overwhelming

Most authors are excellent at writing and are never taught how to market. If any of these worries sound like yours, you are in good company, and there is a calm way forward.

01

"I do not know where to start."

There are hundreds of tips online and many of them disagree. I help you choose a few useful actions in the right order, so you stop guessing.

02

"My book is not selling."

Slow sales usually have a cause, such as an unclear listing, the wrong keywords or readers who never saw the book. We look for the cause before spending anything.

03

"Ads feel confusing and risky."

Advertising has its own language and it is easy to waste money. I explain every term in simple words and set careful limits before anything goes live.

04

"I am not sure who my readers are."

"Everyone" is not a reader. We describe the people most likely to enjoy your book, so every message speaks to someone real.

05

"My book page does not convince."

Visitors decide in seconds. A clear description, the right categories and a focused first impression can help the right readers say yes.

06

"I am afraid of wasting money."

That is a sensible worry. I recommend starting small, measuring honestly and reporting what happened in writing, so you stay in control.

Simple Glossary

Marketing has its own language. Here are the common terms in plain words.

Blurb
The description of your book on its sales page. It helps readers decide whether to buy.
Keywords
Words and phrases readers type into a search box when looking for a book like yours.
Categories
The shelves where your book is placed on a store, such as "Mystery" or "Self-Help".
Metadata
The information that describes your book to stores, including title, description, keywords and categories.
Conversion
When a visitor takes the action you hoped for, such as buying the book or joining your email list.
Impressions
The number of times an ad was shown. It does not mean people clicked on it.
Click-through rate (CTR)
The share of people who saw an ad and clicked it.
ACoS
Advertising Cost of Sales. It compares what you spent on ads with what those ads brought in.
Reader magnet
A free gift, such as a short story or bonus chapter, offered in return for an email address.
Email list
A group of readers who have agreed to receive emails from you.
Launch window
The period around your book's release when you focus extra attention on promotion.
Comparable titles
Published books similar to yours. They help show what readers of your genre already enjoy.
